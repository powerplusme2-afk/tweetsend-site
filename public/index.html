How a send works

Five steps, and only the first one happens on X.

You see a post from someone you want to pay. You reply to it with the bot mention and an amount, for example @TweetSend $50. That is the entire instruction: no address, no network picker, no second app.

TweetSend detects the reply through the X API and identifies the author of the tweet you replied to. You never have to type the recipient's wallet address, or even their handle - the thread already says who it is for.

The bot returns a secure confirmation link. You connect a wallet or use your existing TweetSend balance, pick the supported asset and network if there is a choice, and sign the payment.

If the recipient already uses TweetSend, the funds go to their linked wallet. If they have never used it, the payment stays claimable for that X account instead of sitting at an address nobody has verified.

The recipient signs in with X, proves ownership of the same account the payment was addressed to, and claims the funds to a wallet they control. The bot can report the payment as pending, completed, claimed, expired or refunded along the way.

Short status lines. Reply @TweetSend $25 under a post and the bot answers "Payment ready. Confirm your $25 send." Once it settles it answers "$25 sent to @recipient." If the recipient has not joined yet it answers "$25 is waiting for @recipient. They can claim it by signing in with X." Payments can also report as pending, completed, claimed, expired or refunded, and the site keeps the full history of what you sent, received, claimed and had refunded.
